## The Two Findings That Actually Decide This

### ⚠️ Finding 1: The UltraWeather's treadlife is a real risk

Bridgestone **lowered** the specs from the tire it replaces:

| | WeatherPeak (2022–2026) | **UltraWeather (2026)** |
|---|---|---|
| UTQG treadwear | 700 | **600** ⬇️ |
| Warranty | 70,000 mi | **60,000 mi** ⬇️ |

That's Bridgestone itself signaling a softer, grippier, shorter-lived compound. And the predecessor **already underdelivered badly**: owners who wore WeatherPeaks down to the wear bars averaged roughly **38,600 miles against a 70,000-mile warranty**, with the most common complaint being premature wear. Typical reports ran 38k–50k.

Since UTQG treadwear grades are comparable *within* a brand, a 600 following a 700 that delivered ~40k suggests the UltraWeather realistically returns **40,000–50,000 miles**, not 60,000. Bridgestone's PeakLife polymer is specifically aimed at fixing this, but there is no evidence yet either way.

**Counterpoint:** your conditions are favorable — mild Pacific Northwest climate, a mid-weight sedan, no high-speed or hot-climate abuse. Wear complaints skew toward heavy vehicles and hot regions.

### 💰 Finding 2: The CrossClimate2 costs you real money in fuel

This matters more on your car than on almost any other. Your OE tire was the **Michelin Energy Saver A/S** — a genuine low-rolling-resistance tire — so you're starting from an efficiency baseline.

- **CrossClimate2** measures RRC ~0.009–0.011 and is rated only **"fair"** for rolling resistance by Consumer Reports. Its aggressive V-tread and flexible compound are the cause. Hybrid owners consistently report a **5–10% MPG drop** versus OE LRR tires (a Camry Hybrid owner went 32 → 29 mpg).
- **UltraWeather** is built on **ENLITEN**, explicitly engineered for reduced rolling resistance and hybrid/EV duty — expect roughly a **2%** penalty.

**Fuel cost over 60,000 miles** *(assuming 45 mpg baseline, $4.50/gal)*:

|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (OE LRR) | — | $6,000 | — |
| **UltraWeather** | ~2% | $6,124 | +$124 |
| **CrossClimate2** | ~7% | $6,452 | **+$452** |

> **The CrossClimate2 burns roughly $200–500 more fuel over the life of the tires.** This is the cost nobody mentions, and on a hybrid it partially cancels its treadlife advantage.

### 🕒 Finding 3: At 8,500 miles/year, your tires will **age out before they wear out**

This is decisive. Rubber degrades with time regardless of use — compounds harden and wet grip falls off measurably. **Honda, NHTSA, and most automakers recommend replacement at 6 years**; 10 years is the absolute ceiling, with annual inspection after 5.

At your rate, **6 years ≈ 51,000 miles.** That is your real ceiling — not the warranty.

| Tire | Est. treadlife | Years to wear out | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UltraWeather** | 40–50k | **4.7 – 5.9 yrs** | ✅ **Wears out right at the age limit — near-perfect match** |
| **CrossClimate2** | 55–65k | **6.5 – 7.6 yrs** | ❌ **Ages out with usable tread left** |

> ### 🔑 This eliminates the CrossClimate2's main advantage.

> **And the UltraWeather's biggest risk disappears too.** Even a disappointing 40,000-mile result takes you **4.7 years** — well within a normal replacement cycle. The treadlife question that worried me above is largely academic at your mileage.

**Where:** Costco Issaquah, 1801 10th Ave NW. Cheapest source for *both* tires — $149 under Firestone and $271 under Honda of Bellevue on the UltraWeather.

🚩 **Firestone's "$180 in savings" is really $80** — the extra $100 requires opening a Firestone Credit Card. Costco still wins even if you take the card.

### At the counter
- [ ] 🚩 **Ask them to remove the $11.96 TPMS service fee.** Your 2019 Accord uses **indirect TPMS** — no sensors inside the wheels, nothing to service.
- [ ] 📅 **Check the DOT date code on every tire before they mount them.** This matters more for you than for most buyers. The last four digits of the DOT code are week/year (e.g. `2726` = week 27 of 2026). Since you replace on a *calendar* cycle, a tire that already sat in a warehouse for 18 months costs you ~25% of its usable life. **Insist on tires under 12 months old** — and reject anything over 2 years.
- [ ] ✅ **Verify the sidewall reads `94V`** (or `98V` on the CrossClimate2) — not `H` or `T`.
- [ ] ✅ Confirm stock before driving over.
- [ ] ✅ **Recalibrate TPMS** through the vehicle settings menu afterward — indirect systems require it.
- [ ] 📆 **Write the install date somewhere you'll find it** — at your mileage, the 6-year mark will arrive before the tread does.

### Alignment
**Costco does not perform alignments at any location, including Issaquah** — confirmed. They do tires, balancing, rotation, and flat repair only. (The *Costco Auto Program* offers member discounts at partner shops, which is likely what you saw.)

- Get a **free alignment check** at Big O Tires (60 NW Gilman Blvd) or Les Schwab.
- Pay for it **only if** it's actually out of spec, or if your current tires show **uneven shoulder wear** or the car pulls. Roughly **$89–129**.
- ❌ Don't reflexively pay Honda's $149.95.

### No spare tire
Add a **plug kit + 12V compressor (~$70)** to the trunk, or a **Modern Spare kit (~$430)** that drops into the empty factory wheel well. AAA will not plug a tire roadside — with no spare, they tow.
