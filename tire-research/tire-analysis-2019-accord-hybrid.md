## 4. Review Analysis — The ≥50k Warranty Shortlist

### 🥇 Michelin CrossClimate2 — 60k · $1,011 OTD · **3PMSF**

**Strengths**
- Consistently rated **class-leading or near-leading for wet braking** — the single most important metric for Puget Sound driving.
- **3PMSF severe-snow certified.** Confident traction in light-to-moderate snow and slush; short stopping distances; stable cornering.
- Long-term owner tests (18k–31k miles) show **even wear**, with real-world projections of 60k–75k miles. One 31k-mile test still measured ~6.5mm remaining.
- Genuinely a one-tire-year-round solution — no seasonal swap needed.

**Weaknesses (real, and repeatedly reported)**
- **Fuel economy penalty of roughly 1–3 MPG**, and reviewers specifically note the hit is *larger on hybrids and EVs*. This is the most relevant downside for your Accord Hybrid. Budget ~3–6% rolling-resistance cost.
- **Directional tread = front-to-back rotation only.** Cannot cross-rotate, so heel/toe "sawtooth" shoulder wear develops and is irreversible. This is the root cause of the noise complaints.
- **Growing road noise after ~20k miles** — some owners describe a drone or harmonic hum, occasionally misdiagnosed as a wheel bearing.
- Reports of **soft sidewalls** (occasional bubbling/bulging after pothole strikes) and **tramlining** — following ruts and road grooves, with vague steering. More pronounced as the tire wears.
- Firmer ride than a dedicated touring all-season.
- Isolated NHTSA complaints of rapid tread/sidewall degradation.
- Most expensive of the shortlist, and **not currently on promotion**.

## 5. The Spare Tire Question 🔧

### Your current situation
- The 2019 Accord Hybrid ships with **no spare** — only a sealant/inflator kit (the hybrid battery packaging drove this).
- **There IS an empty wheel well under the trunk floor** sized for a spare. Nothing needs to be fabricated.
- You have **AAA**.

### What AAA actually does with no spare
This is the critical detail: **AAA does not plug or patch tires at the roadside.** Their flat-tire service is fundamentally "swap to your spare." With no spare, **they tow you.** That means:
- You're waiting for a tow truck, not back on the road in 20 minutes.
- Your car goes to a shop, on that shop's schedule — potentially overnight if it's a Sunday or you're on I-90 east of the pass.
- It works, but it is a **slow** resolution, not a fast one.

### Downsides of the factory sealant kit
- Single-use and temporary; only handles **small tread punctures** — useless for sidewall damage or large holes.
- **Sealant can clog or corrode TPMS hardware**, and shops often charge extra to clean a sealant-filled tire before patching. Some refuse to repair it at all.
- Canisters expire and need periodic replacement.

| Complete kit (jack, cross wrench, hold-down hardware, disposal bag) | **$429.95** |

Engineered for Accord brake caliper clearance and correct rolling diameter (important — a mismatched diameter stresses the hybrid drivetrain and ABS). Front or rear use.

**Trade-off:** ~40 lbs of permanent weight, which will cost you a small amount of MPG in a hybrid, plus the loss of that under-floor storage cubby.

### Option B — Plug kit + 12V compressor 💡 **Best value**
~$60–80 total. A quality tire plug kit and a compact inflator handle the overwhelming majority of real-world flats (tread punctures from nails/screws) **in place, in about 15 minutes**, with no sealant contamination and no tow. Fits in the cubby with room to spare and adds ~5 lbs.

This does not cover sidewall damage or a blowout — but neither does a sealant kit, and for those cases AAA tows you regardless.

### Option C — Run-flat tires (DriveGuard Plus) ❌ **Not recommended for you**

| Cost of run-flat vs. Ultraweather | $1,015.52 − $829.88 = **+$185.64** |
|---|---|
| Cost of run-flat vs. CrossClimate2 | $1,015.52 − $1,011.10 = **+$4.42** |

Arguments against:
1. **🚩 Your Accord has INDIRECT TPMS.** The 2019 Accord (like all 2013–2019 Accords) has **no pressure sensors in the wheels** — it infers pressure loss from ABS wheel-speed differences. Bridgestone requires DriveGuard to be fitted only to TPMS-equipped vehicles precisely because *a deflated run-flat feels normal to drive*. An indirect system is inherently less sensitive and slower to trigger than a direct one, and it needs manual recalibration after every pressure change or rotation. You are relying on the weakest form of warning for the tire type that most depends on that warning.
2. **No 3PMSF rating** — you'd give up all-weather capability.
3. **65k warranty but worse real-world tread life** than conventional touring tires, plus a fuel-economy penalty on a hybrid.
4. **Redundant with what you already have:** AAA + Costco's 5-year road hazard warranty + Costco's free lifetime flat repair.
5. Firmer ride, and replacement run-flats are expensive and less widely stocked if you need one on a road trip.

> **Recommendation: buy the CrossClimate2 or Ultraweather + a $430 Modern Spare kit (or a $70 plug kit).** For roughly the price difference of the run-flats you get a *universal* solution that also protects you against sidewall damage and blowouts — which run-flats handle, but which a spare handles better and cheaper.

## 8. Alignment ⚙️

**Costco does not perform wheel alignments** — at any location, as a matter of policy (equipment cost, specialized technician requirements, and liability). So this has to be handled separately.

### Should you do one with new tires?
**Not automatically — but check it.** An alignment is *strongly recommended* if any of these apply:
- Your **current tires show uneven wear** (inner/outer shoulder wearing faster) — this is the clearest signal, and it means new tires will wear the same way.
- The car **pulls to one side**, the steering wheel is off-center, or it feels unstable.
- You've **hit a significant pothole or curb**, or had suspension work.
- It's been **over a year** since the last alignment.

Skipping it when it's needed causes rapid uneven wear on brand-new tires, reduced fuel economy, and — importantly — **can void the mileage/treadwear warranty**, since those warranties exclude abnormal wear caused by misalignment. On a 7-year-old car with Pacific Northwest potholes, an alignment check is cheap insurance on an $800–1,000 tire investment.

### Where to get it (cheapest first)

|---|---|---|

## 10. Regional Context — Why Wet Grip Wins Here 🌧️

- Western Washington lowlands: **rain is the dominant hazard**, not snow. Wet braking and hydroplaning resistance should be the primary selection criteria. This is what disqualifies the `B`-traction EverDrive.
- **Snoqualmie Pass / WSDOT rules:** when "Traction Tires Required" is posted, **M+S or All-Season tires legally qualify** provided they have ≥1/8" (4/32") of tread. **3PMSF is *not* legally required.** All eleven Costco options are M+S-branded and therefore compliant when new.
  - However, when **"Chains Required"** is posted, chains are mandatory for passenger vehicles regardless of tire type (your FWD Accord has no AWD exemption).
  - So 3PMSF isn't about legality — it's about **actual capability** in slush, cold rain, and the surprise Issaquah snow event. It also keeps you safe on the pass in conditions where M+S tires are legal but genuinely out of their depth.
- **Verdict:** if you ever drive east on I-90 in winter, or want to not think about the two or three annual Eastside snow days, 3PMSF (CrossClimate2 or Ultraweather) is worth it. If you never leave the lowlands, a top-tier wet-focused all-season would also serve.
