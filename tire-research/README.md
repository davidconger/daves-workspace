**Recommendation: Bridgestone UltraWeather, $829.88.**

The reasoning that actually mattered, and the part worth remembering for the next car:

**Usage rate changed the answer.** At roughly 8,500 miles a year, these tires **age out
before they wear out**. Rubber hardens with time regardless of mileage, and Honda, NHTSA
and most automakers advise replacement at six years, ten being the ceiling. That single
input reframed the whole comparison — a longer treadlife warranty is worth little if the
tread will never be the thing that expires.

Which is why the premium option lost. The Michelin CrossClimate2 costs about $180 more
and carries a real fuel-economy penalty on a hybrid, and neither of those buys anything
back on a set that will be replaced on a calendar rather than on wear.

The load and speed rating are worth checking on any quote: OE here is **94V**, and some
otherwise-attractive listings come in below the speed rating the car was built around.
