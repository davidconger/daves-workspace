# Bailey Zimmerman & Chandler Walters @ Washington State Fair — Caption Ideas

*Show was Thu, Sep 17, 2026 · Columbia Bank Grandstand, Washington State Fair, Puyallup · Chandler Walters opening for Bailey Zimmerman · doors 6:30, show 7:30, rain or shine · for 98.9 The Bull (Country) · shot by @dcongerphoto (confirm before posting if someone else shot this one)*

This is a genuinely different night than the last three Fair country shows. The PRCA Cinch Playoff Wild Card rodeo wrapped September 13, so there's no arena floor, no dirt, no Chute bar this time — Bailey Zimmerman and Chandler Walters are playing a normal seated Grandstand show, same as the room Culture Club opened the series in two weeks earlier. That actually makes this easier: none of the boots-in-the-dirt material from the Gretchen Wilson, Dylan Marlowe or Chase Rice posts applies here, so the two files below lean on completely different material — Zimmerman's own catalog and the calendar, Walters' songwriting résumé and his steel guitar.

One correction worth flagging up front: Zimmerman's new album is literally called *Different Night Same Rodeo*, which is a tempting title given three straight rodeo-themed posts already in this folder. I left it out on purpose. Using it here would be the one title in his catalog that actively duplicates the theme you asked to avoid, even though the words "different night" are doing the opposite in his case. Skip it.

## Chandler Walters (opener)

He's not a name The Bull's audience knows yet, so every option below carries the same anchor: before Chandler Walters was a solo artist, he was the Grammy-nominated co-writer of Post Malone and Morgan Wallen's "I Had Some Help," a number-one hit on the Hot 100. That's the fact that turns an unfamiliar name into "oh, that guy" for a casual listener, so it needs to survive a skim in every version, per the rule for a thin anchor.

> ⭐ Before he ever opened a show of his own, @chandlerwaltersmusic already had some help writing a number one. 🎸 Tonight the steel guitar came out for him, and Puyallup got Under the Neon a full year before it hits the radio. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Had some help" is a direct, gentle nod to "I Had Some Help," the Post Malone/Morgan Wallen song he co-wrote — it topped the Hot 100, so it's real recognition even though Walters never sang a note of it himself. Stating it as his own writing credit rather than implying he performed it keeps this accurate. "Under the Neon" is his own recent single (out this past July), and honky-tonk neon signs are exactly the kind of lit-up-at-night image a Grandstand opening slot delivers, so the title reads as a literal description of the stage as much as a song name.

> He wrote the words to a number one before he ever picked up a mic of his own. 🎶 @chandlerwaltersmusic brought the steel guitar that made him TikTok-famous to @wastatefairec, and Puyallup was Worth The Trouble of finding out who he is. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Worth the Trouble" is one of his own singles, and framing it as the crowd's payoff for showing up early works because it's genuinely true of most opening slots — the audience has to make an effort to care, and this one rewards it. The pedal steel detail is accurate and specific: it's literally what got him noticed on TikTok before ERNEST brought him into his band.

> Before "I Had Some Help" ever hit number one, somebody had to write it. 🎸 That somebody, @chandlerwaltersmusic, opened for @baileyzimmerman at @wastatefairec and gave Puyallup Come On Whiskey to start the night right. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Come On Whiskey" is another of his own titles, plain English and an easy invitation-to-the-night line for an opening set. This version puts the Post Malone/Wallen credit first and by name, which is the most explicit version of the anchor if the room needs more convincing than the others.

> Short: The Grammy-nominated pen behind a Post Malone/Morgan Wallen number one, live with a pedal steel at @wastatefairec. 🎸 @chandlerwaltersmusic opened for @baileyzimmerman and Puyallup got there early for once. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

Good for a tight stage shot where there isn't room for a second song title — the songwriting credit alone is doing all the recognition work here.

## Bailey Zimmerman (headliner)

He has four Country Airplay number ones off his debut album alone — "Fall in Love," "Rock and a Hard Place," "Religiously" and "Where It Ends" — so there's no anchor anxiety here, only a choice of which one to lead with.

> ⭐ Puyallup did what the season already had planned. 🍂 @baileyzimmerman brought "Fall in Love" to @wastatefairec on a September night, and the whole Grandstand meant it Religiously. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Fall in Love" is his 2022 debut single and first-ever number one, the fastest debut to top Country Airplay that year — that's the anchor, and the double meaning is free: it's literally fall in Puyallup on September 17. "Religiously" is his second number one, about loving someone completely and without fail rather than anything to do with actual church, so "meant it religiously" reads as devoted, not literal — worth knowing the difference before you publish it. 🍂 in place of the hat or boot emoji used on the last three Fair posts, both because it's genuinely autumn and because it keeps this file visually distinct from the rodeo run.

> @baileyzimmerman took the Grandstand stage at @wastatefairec with no rodeo dirt under his boots this time, just four number ones and a crowd that knew every word to Rock and a Hard Place. 🔥 Puyallup didn't need convincing. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Rock and a Hard Place" is his second Country Airplay number one, reached less than four months after "Fall in Love" — one of the fastest two-number-one runs by a new artist in nearly 25 years, so naming it alongside "four number ones" is accurate rather than exaggerated. The "no rodeo dirt" line does the differentiation work directly: it tells the reader this is a different kind of Fair night without borrowing any of the dirt-floor imagery itself.

> Illinois farm kid to a sold-out Grandstand at @wastatefairec. 🎤 @baileyzimmerman played Where It Ends and Puyallup answered like it never wanted the set to. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Where It Ends" is his fourth Country Airplay number one off the same debut album, and pairing his small-town Louisville, Illinois background with a sentence about the set never wanting to end is a gentle, defensible contrast rather than a stretch — he really did go from a rural upbringing and blue-collar jobs straight into four straight number ones.

> Short: Four number ones deep and still Falling in Love with a new crowd every night. 🍂 @baileyzimmerman closed out @wastatefairec's Grandstand and Puyallup sang every one back. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

One title, kept simple for a portrait or tight stage frame, with the same seasonal 🍂 as the recommended pick.
