# Role Model @ WaMu Theater — Caption Ideas

*Show was Fri, Sep 11, 2026 · WaMu Theater, Seattle · Chuck on Tour, with Samia · for MOViN 92.5 (Top 40) · shot by @dcongerphoto*

Back to Top 40 weighting after two country nights, which changes what counts as recognizable. For MOViN's audience the metric is streaming and TikTok reach, not Country Airplay, and by that measure Role Model has exactly one song that is unmissable right now.

There is also a geography gift sitting here that this folder has cashed once already and should cash again.

---

## ⭐ The pick

> @rolemodel brought Chuck on Tour from the Pine Tree State to the Evergreen State, and @wamutheater met him the whole way. 🌲 Seattle showed up with High Hopes 3000, got Joy in return, then completely lost the plot for Sally, When the Wine Runs Out. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Pine Tree State to Evergreen State** is the same move that made the Noah Kahan caption the best-performing post in this folder, and it works for the same reason: Tucker Pillsbury is from Maine, he started this tour at Thompson's Point in Portland, and the two states have nicknames that rhyme conceptually. Spelling out both nicknames rather than saying "Maine to Seattle" is what makes it a line instead of a fact. Two evergreen-adjacent states, one guy, one long flight.

**Sally, When the Wine Runs Out** is the anchor and it has to be last in the sentence, because it is the one the algorithm and the casual scroller both recognize. It broke on TikTok on the strength of the line dance, and he performed it on SNL with a Charli XCX cameo. If a reader knows one Role Model song in September 2026 it is this one, which is exactly the license the other two titles need.

**High Hopes 3000** and **Joy** are the first two singles from *Chuck Timely & the Hourglass*, out August 7, 2026 — so this is the current record and these are the songs actually being pushed. "Showed up with High Hopes 3000, got Joy in return" is the construction I am happiest with in this file: both titles are used as plain nouns in a sentence that would still parse if you had never heard of either one. That is the standard to hold titles to.

**"Completely lost the plot"** is doing the crowd-reaction work without claiming anything unverifiable. It is also the register Role Model's audience actually uses about his shows.

**Hashtag is deliberately absent.** ⚠️ #ChuckOnTour is the obvious construction from the tour name, but I could not confirm it is genuinely in circulation, and this folder's rule is that an invented hashtag is worse than none. The caption is complete without one. If you can see it in use on his own posts, drop it in after the last title and before the credit.

## The Kansas one

> Not in Kansas anymore, not in Maine either. 🌲 @rolemodel took Chuck on Tour into @wamutheater and Seattle sang Sally, When the Wine Runs Out loud enough to be heard in the parking lot. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

***Kansas Anymore*** is his 2024 album and the title is already a fragment of the most famous line in American film, which means you can use it without the reader needing to know it is an album at all. The joke then extends it — not Kansas, not Maine, Seattle — so the geography and the title-drop are the same move. Tighter than the pick and better for a square crop or a tight portrait.

## For the openers

Samia is the confirmed support on this run and she is not filler — she has her own strong following, and tagging support acts reliably earns a repost. Earlier sourcing had Keni Titus on the bill too, but only Samia is confirmed first-party by WaMu Theater and Live Nation, so tag her alone unless you saw otherwise.

> Two sets, one room, zero misses. 🌲 Samia warmed @wamutheater up and @rolemodel closed it out with Joy, Chasing Midnight and a Sally, When the Wine Runs Out that nobody sat down for. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Chasing Midnight** is another *Chuck Timely & the Hourglass* cut and it fits a late-set slot in a way that reads naturally even to someone who does not know it. Only run this version if you actually shot Samia — claiming two sets on a photo of one is the kind of small dishonesty that gets noticed.

## Short

> @rolemodel and a room at @wamutheater that knew every word to Sally, When the Wine Runs Out before the first chorus. 🌲 Chuck on Tour is a good time, Seattle. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

One title, one anchor, done. Use it when the image is strong enough that the caption's only job is to stay out of the way.

## Notes before you post

**Two songs in his catalog cannot go on a Top 40 station feed.** ⚠️ **If Jesus Saves, She's My Type** is a religion-and-sex joke in the title alone, and **Death Wish** is exactly the kind of title that reads as a statement rather than a reference when it sits next to a photo. Both are real songs with real fans and neither belongs in a brand caption. Same call as *Sin Pijama* on the Becky G post.

**Chuck Timely is a character, not a person to tag.** The album is *Chuck Timely & the Hourglass* and the tour is Chuck on Tour, both built around an invented persona. Using "Chuck" as shorthand for the tour is fine — "brought Chuck on Tour," "Chuck on Tour is a good time" — but do not write anything that implies Chuck is a collaborator or a real guest.

**His name is Tucker Pillsbury and I did not use it.** Deliberate. The Maine detail is the useful part of the biography; the government name is trivia, and Top 40 captions are not the place for it.

**Do not reach for Rx.** His 2022 debut album has real fans but nothing on it competes with *Sally* for recognition, and per the anchor rule, spending a title slot on a deep cut only works once the recognizable one has already landed. Every option above spends its first slot correctly.

**The Maine angle is now two for two in this folder.** Noah Kahan, now Role Model. Worth noticing that it works because it is a compliment to the crowd's city, not just a fact about the artist's — the line always has to end up in Seattle.
