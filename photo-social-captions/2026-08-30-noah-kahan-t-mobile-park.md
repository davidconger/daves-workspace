Three changes you made to my draft, all of them improvements worth keeping as rules:

**The tour hashtag.** `#TheGreatDivideTour` sits after the caption and before the photo credit. That's a discovery channel none of the earlier posts used, and on a tour hashtag people are actively browsing, which is a plausible part of why this one traveled. Worth pulling the official tour name for every show from here on.

**Two emoji instead of one.** 💚 🌲 paired together hit harder than either alone, and they're doing different jobs. The green heart carries the affection and the tree carries the place, and stacked at the pivot point between the two sentences they act as one beat rather than clutter.

**You picked the run-on.** The long unbroken first sentence was the option I flagged for wide crowd frames, and on a night with a massive crowd that was the right read.

## ⭐ The pick, with a legibility fix

Your instinct to question the green states line is right. Here it is with the reference made explicit:

> Green Mountain State to Evergreen State, one Northern Attitude. 🌲 It's not Stick Season in Seattle, but @tmobilepark sang along to @noahkahanmusic like We'll All Be Here Forever. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

And your original, for comparison:

> Two green states, one Northern Attitude. 🌲 It's not Stick Season in Seattle, but @tmobilepark sang along to @noahkahanmusic like We'll All Be Here Forever. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

### Why the original is a deep cut

It asks the reader to know three things at once. That Washington is the Evergreen State, which most Seattle people do know. That Vermont is the Green Mountain State, which is common knowledge in New England and close to unknown outside it. And that Noah Kahan is from Vermont, which his actual fans know cold but a casual Top 40 listener who picked up Stick Season off the radio may not.

The real problem is that "two green states" never names either state, so there's no thread to pull. A reader who doesn't already have the pieces has nothing to reason from, and a caption that needs a footnote has failed at being a caption. 🤔

Naming both states fixes it without losing anything. "Green Mountain State to Evergreen State" is legible to someone with zero context, since it plainly reads as two green places, and the "to" quietly does the work of saying he traveled here. Fans still get the Vermont layer on top. You keep the "one Northern Attitude" turn, which was the elegant part.

If you want it even flatter, this version assumes nothing at all:

> Vermont green, Seattle green, one Northern Attitude. 🌲 It's not Stick Season at @tmobilepark, but Seattle sang along to @noahkahanmusic like We'll All Be Here Forever. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

### Why the titles work

Three titles, and every one earns its spot. **Northern Attitude** was the second single off *Stick Season*, and it's literally about the closed-off heart he grew up with in Strafford, Vermont, a town of under two thousand people, asking to be forgiven for it. Seattle being the northernmost major city in the lower forty-eight makes that land differently here than it would anywhere else. **Stick Season** is the hit doing your Top 40 work, number nine on the Hot 100 and number two on the Billboard 200. **We'll All Be Here Forever** is the deluxe album title, which is why it works so well over a crowd frame. Album titles tend to be complete thoughts in a way song titles aren't.

Everything below is a variation on cadence rather than an argument that the pick needs fixing.

## Fragments, then a long run

The staccato open reads fast on a phone and buys you a payoff line.

> 🌲 No rain, No Complaints, just @noahkahanmusic and a Northern Attitude. @tmobilepark sang Stick Season back loud enough to hear it in Vermont. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

Leading with the emoji turns it into a bullet point your eye lands on before the words start.

I had written "no rain, no Stick Season" here and you were right to stop on it. In a concert caption, "no Stick Season" reads first as *he didn't play Stick Season*, which is both wrong and the last thing you'd want to imply about his biggest song. I meant it as "it isn't that season yet," but the sentence doesn't carry that, and a caption you have to argue for is broken. **No Complaints** replaces it. It's a real track off the deluxe, it's a natural English phrase, and "no rain, no complaints" scans without any ambiguity at all. Then **Stick Season** comes back later in the caption used the plain way, as the song the crowd sang, which is unambiguous and true. ✅

Same staccato rhythm, emoji parked at the back instead:

> Wrong season. Right city. Right ballpark. @noahkahanmusic brought a Northern Attitude to @tmobilepark and Seattle handed it straight back. 🌲 (📸 @dcongerphoto)

"Wrong season" is only fair because **Stick Season** names a specific stretch of late New England fall, after Halloween and before the snow. In August it genuinely isn't that season, so the line is accurate rather than cute. Note this version says "wrong season" and not "no Stick Season," which is why it avoids the problem above.

## One long sentence, no stopping

Sometimes the run-on is the point, especially under a wide crowd frame where the photo already did the work.

> @noahkahanmusic turned @tmobilepark into a Vermont field for one night, Northern Attitude and all. 💚 Seattle sang Stick Season back like We'll All Be Here Forever. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

Dropping the emoji between the two sentences makes it act as the pivot. Worth checking on this one: "a Vermont field" is doing metaphor work, not claiming anything factual about the show, and **We'll All Be Here Forever** stays attached to the crowd rather than to him, which is the honest reading of a deluxe album title about a place and its people.

## Long setup, short punch

Inverting it puts the memorable line last, which is where people stop scrolling.

> @noahkahanmusic came three thousand miles from a Vermont town of two thousand people, and @tmobilepark sang Stick Season loud enough that nobody left Homesick. Northern Attitude, meet the Evergreen State. 🌲 (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Homesick** is the one to sanity check here, and it survives. The song is about being stuck in the place you're from rather than missing it, so using it as "nobody left homesick" flips it deliberately rather than misreading it. If that bothers you, the Ballpark version below uses it the straight way.

## Warmer, if the frames are warm

> Seattle gave @noahkahanmusic Everywhere, Everything tonight. 🌲 A Northern Attitude that finally found its people at @tmobilepark. (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Everywhere, Everything** is the Gracie Abrams duet, the one that ends up on wedding playlists, so it carries recognition even though it charted well below the singles.

This one originally read "Not Stick Season, not Homesick, just a Northern Attitude," which trips the same wire as the caption above. Negated titles read as songs he skipped. Cut, rather than rewritten, because the line is tighter without them anyway.

## Why these titles

**Stick Season** is the whole Top 40 argument by itself. Number nine on the Hot 100, number two on the Billboard 200, and a Best New Artist nomination out of it. Once it's in the sentence, every other title is free to be chosen for the idea rather than the chart, which is why **Northern Attitude** and **We'll All Be Here Forever** get to do the thematic work.

The green states line is the piece I'd protect. Vermont is the Green Mountain State, Washington is the Evergreen State, he called the album a love letter to New England, and *Northern Attitude* is literally about a cold, closed-off upbringing in a Vermont town of under two thousand people. Seattle is the northernmost major city in the lower forty-eight. That parallel is true, it's specific, and nobody else covering this show will write it. 🌲

Also worth saying out loud: stick season is the stretch after Halloween before the snow arrives, so in late August it is factually the wrong season. Naming that is more interesting than ignoring it, and it keeps you clear of the weather entirely on a dry night.
