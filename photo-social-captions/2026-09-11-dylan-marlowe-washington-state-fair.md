# Dylan Marlowe @ Washington State Fair — Caption Ideas

*Show was Fri, Sep 11, 2026 · **Dancin' in the Dirt**, Columbia Bank Grandstand arena floor, Washington State Fair, Puyallup · follows the PRCA Cinch Playoff Wild Card rodeo · for 98.9 The Bull (Country) · shot by @dcongerphoto*

Same event as the Gretchen Wilson night — rodeo runs, rodeo ends, crowd walks out onto the arena dirt, standing room only, late. Different problem though. Wilson has a five-times-platinum album to anchor on. Marlowe is early enough in his career that he has exactly **one** title most of the Bull's listeners will recognize on sight, so the pick has to build around it rather than assume it.

The good news is that the coincidence here is almost too convenient: **he named an EP *Dirt Road When I Die*, and the Fair booked him onto a floor made of dirt.** That is the post.

---

## ⭐ The pick

> He named an EP Dirt Road When I Die, so @wastatefairec went ahead and handed @dylanmarlowe an arena floor made of the stuff. 🥾 Dancin' in the Dirt sang Boys Back Home back at him like Puyallup was the home in question. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Dirt Road When I Die** is his 2023 EP, and this is the rare case where the joke needs no setup at all — you state the title, you state the booking, and the reader does the rest. "Went ahead and" is what sells it, because it implies the Fair noticed too. That is a much better sentence than announcing the coincidence outright.

**Boys Back Home** is the anchor and currently the only one available. It is his debut single, a duet with Dylan Scott, and it climbed to the top 25 at country radio — the one song where a Bull listener scrolling past will go *oh, that one.* The rule for this batch is that at least one title has to be recognizable so the audience understands the rest are titles too, and this is carrying that load by itself.

**"Like Puyallup was the home in question"** is the line doing the real work. The song is about the friends you left behind in your hometown; Marlowe's is Statesboro, Georgia, which is about as far from Puyallup as the contiguous states allow. Letting a Washington crowd briefly claim it is a small, warm joke, and it is the kind of thing that reads as affection for the crowd rather than a stat.

**🥾** over 🤠 on this one. The hat emoji is right for Gretchen Wilson's night; the boot is right here because the Fair's own advisory literally tells people what shoes to wear, and because Marlowe's whole lane is dirt roads rather than rodeo.

## The one for the radio feed itself

**Shop Radio** is an album cut off *Mid-Twenties Crisis* about the radio playing in a garage or work shop — the exact listening situation 98.9 The Bull is built for. This is the most station-specific caption in the batch and I would use it on The Bull's own feed over anything else here.

> Somewhere a shop radio is playing this guy, and last night @dylanmarlowe played it himself on the dirt at @wastatefairec. 🥾 Boys Back Home into a floor full of people who knew every word. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

The lowercase "shop radio" in the first clause is deliberate — it reads as a plain description, and only lands as a title-drop on the second pass. That is a better version of the trick than capitalizing it and demanding the reader notice.

## If you want the chickens

This is real and it is the single most memorable thing about him: **Marlowe travels with chickens.** He has brought them along on tour and posts them constantly. The Washington State Fair has full livestock barns two hundred yards from that stage, which makes this the only venue on earth where the bit is genuinely relevant.

> Only artist at @wastatefairec who could have wandered over to the livestock barns and found family. 🐔 @dylanmarlowe brought Boys Back Home to the Dancin' in the Dirt crowd and Puyallup did not let him finish a single chorus alone. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

Funny, specific, and completely useless if the audience does not already follow him — which is why it is here as an option and not the pick. Use it if the post is going somewhere his fans will see it. Do not use it as the main Bull post, where most of the feed is meeting him for the first time.

## The straight one

> Statesboro, Georgia to a dirt floor in Puyallup. 🥾 @dylanmarlowe played Boys Back Home, Heart Brakes and Stick To My Guns at @wastatefairec, and Dancin' in the Dirt sent it all straight back at him. #DoThePuyallup (📸 @dcongerphoto)

**Heart Brakes** and **Stick To My Guns** are both off *Mid-Twenties Crisis* (2024), the second featuring Riley Green. Three titles is the ceiling and this hits it, so this version only works if the photo is wide and the post is functioning as a setlist recap rather than a moment. The geography line does the same Pine-Tree-to-Evergreen job that worked on the Noah Kahan post, just with a much longer distance.

## Notes before you post

**Dylan Scott was not there.** ⚠️ *Boys Back Home* is a duet and the temptation is to write something like "the Dylans" or imply a guest spot. Do not — it was Marlowe's night alone. Every option above credits the song to him without naming Scott, which is accurate and avoids a correction in the comments.

**Careful with *Mid-Twenties Crisis* as a description of him.** It is a great album title and a fine song title, but he is twenty-nine now, so writing "a mid-twenties crisis on the dirt" would be making a claim about his age that is a year or three stale. Fine to name as a title, not fine to use as a characterization. I kept it to a title-drop in the last option only.

**Do not over-lean on 80 Acres.** He founded a country-punk side band under that name in 2026, which is genuinely interesting, but it is a different project and naming it in a caption about a country radio set will confuse more people than it delights.

**He is also a hit songwriter, which is the best fact in reserve.** He co-wrote Jon Pardi's **Last Night Lonely**, a number one. None of the captions above use it because it pulls focus toward a song he did not sing, but if a follow-up post needs a fresh angle, "the guy who wrote Last Night Lonely" is a strong, checkable credential for exactly this audience.

**Anchor check.** This is the thinnest anchor of the five files in this batch — *Boys Back Home* at top 25 is real but it is not *Redneck Woman*. That is why every single option above names it, including the short ones. If one title has to survive the reader skimming, it has to be that one.

**His handle is @dylanmarlowe, and his own website gets it wrong.** ⚠️ dylanmarlowe.com links out to `instagram.com/dylanmarlowemusic`, which **does not exist** — the link is stale. The live account is **@dylanmarlowe**, verified badge, 228K followers, bio "@80acresmusic / Proverbs 16:3." This is a good reminder that an artist's own site is not automatically authoritative on their own handle.

**His current single is NO COINCIDENCE.** None of the options above use it, because it is too new to function as an anchor and the whole file is already leaning on *Boys Back Home*. But it is what he is promoting, so if this post is going anywhere his label will see it, working the title in is a cheap goodwill move. "No coincidence he got the dirt floor" writes itself against the *Dirt Road When I Die* joke.

**The Fair's handle is @wastatefairec, and this one was wrong until now.** ⚠️ The obvious `@thefair` belongs to a private individual with 119 followers and one post. The real account is **@wastatefairec** — verified, 34K followers, bio begins "Official account," linked from the footer of thefair.com. The "ec" is for Washington State Fair Event Center, the org's formal name. Earlier posts in this folder used `@thefair` and have been corrected; if any of them are already live, edit the caption.

**Tag @puyalluprodeo too.** The PRCA Cinch Playoffs Wild Card at Puyallup Rodeo runs September 10-13 and its account's own bio points at @wastatefairec. On a Dancin' in the Dirt post you are standing on their dirt, so tagging them is both accurate and an easy repost.
